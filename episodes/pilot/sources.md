# Sources — How Rome Printed Itself Broke

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Augustan denarius was roughly 95%+ silver | Kenneth W. Harl, *Coinage in the Roman Economy, 300 B.C. to A.D. 700* (Johns Hopkins, 1996), ch. 4; Wikipedia, "Denarius" |
| Nero's reform (c. AD 64) cut the denarius's weight and silver content | Harl (1996); Wikipedia, "Denarius" |
| Silver content fell to ~75% under Marcus Aurelius and ~50% under Septimius Severus | Harl (1996); D. R. Walker, *The Metrology of the Roman Silver Coinage* (BAR, 1976–78) |
| By c. AD 268–270 the antoninianus held under ~5% silver | Harl (1996); Wikipedia, "Antoninianus" |
| Diocletian's Edict on Maximum Prices (AD 301) set price caps with death as the penalty and was widely ignored | Lactantius, *De Mortibus Persecutorum* 7; Wikipedia, "Edict on Maximum Prices" |

Chart values are rounded estimates; studies differ by a few percentage points, so the on-screen footnote labels them approximate.
